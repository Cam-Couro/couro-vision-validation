Couro vs Vicon — ASPset data expansion lifts r to 0.83

v12 · 2026-05-28 · OpenCap (9 subj, drop jumps) + ASPset-510 (17 subj, 30 athletic actions) · 26 subjects · 1,680 trials · LOSO cross-validated

Data tripled, accuracy jumped. Added 17 new subjects across 30 sport actions (jumps, throws, sprints, kicks) from the ASPset-510 dataset. Processed through Couro's RTMPose-X Halpe-26 on AWS A10G GPU (1,410 video clips). Combined with the existing OpenCap drop-jump trials.

23 of 25 deploy-ready slots, up from 22 (v9/v10). And the strong deploys got dramatically stronger: 9 slots now correlate with Vicon at r ≥ 0.70 (vs only 1 before).

Best result: hip flexion ROM at r = 0.83 from oblique camera angles. Front-view metrics that were previously weak (hip flexion front_center r=0.20) are now strong (r=0.78). The data was the binding constraint, not the algorithm.

8. What's still left as a real lever

  1. Sport-specific data collection — ASPset is generic athletic motions. AUSL softball pitching, Sharks hockey skating, etc. need their own validation. Custom mocap collection or AthletePose3D commercial license.
  2. Better foot keypoints — for the 2 still-failing ankle slots, the limitation is RTMPose-X foot/heel keypoint quality. DWPose or a sports-specific pose model with explicit foot supervision could help.
  3. Multi-camera fusion — out of scope per product constraints, but would push best r toward 0.90+.

The big algorithmic story is done. The remaining wins come from sport-specific data and (selectively) a better keypoint model.
