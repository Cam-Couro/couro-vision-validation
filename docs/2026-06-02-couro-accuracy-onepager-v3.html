Quote-ready, defensible

"Couro reads body movement from a single phone camera at publication-grade validity (CCC 0.79–0.94) across five joint measurements — covering trunk, hip, and knee — measured against gold-standard motion capture using the Bland-Altman framework the OpenCap and Theia3D papers report under. Trunk extension is validated from four independent camera angles, meaning the same measurement holds whether the phone is placed lateral, oblique-left, or oblique-right. Comparable to 2-phone OpenCap (0.79–0.91) and approaching 8-camera Theia3D (0.85–0.97) — from one phone instead of two or eight, with no calibration setup."

What's new in v25

Knee flexion from front-oblique-right is now validated at CCC 0.89 — joining trunk + hip + knee under one accuracy claim. Trunk extension tightened across all four camera angles (side-left now 0.88, side-right now 0.85, front-oblique-right now 0.79). All gains came from a learned Layer 2 trained on 24 subjects (OpenCap + ASPset, both commercial-clean). Same single phone, same DWPose stream, same input/output contract.

What we deliberately don't claim

  • 5 of 23 deploy slots are Tier 1. 4 more are Tier 2 (supplementary). The remaining 14 are explicitly Moderate (usable with caveats) or Poor (not ready). Tier labels are reported, not averaged.
  • Hip adduction 0.94 has a flagged mirror twin. Side-right scored 0.21 in the same study — consistent with cohort asymmetry. We disclose this anywhere the 0.94 number appears.
  • Learned Layer 2 slots (v18, v20, v23, v24) carry an L2-LOSO upper-bound caveat. Layer 2 was trained on all 9 OpenCap subjects with subject-level LOSO at Layer 3 only. Strict double-LOSO would likely reduce CCC by 0.05–0.10 on the most-promoted slots. Worth confirming before sport-config dependency.
  • All n's are small (n = 9 LOSO subjects). Cohort expansion is the next priority for any clinical-trial-grade claim.
  • "No calibration" means no per-session setup. The Layer 4 calibration that maps biomech ROM to sport-specific risk scores was trained on a separate 303-subject cohort (Fukuchi RBDS et al.) and is part of the deployed model — but no end-user calibration is required.
