What this document claims, in one paragraph

Couro takes a single phone video, runs a pose-estimation pipeline, reconstructs joint angles in 3D, predicts range of motion (ROM) per trial, and outputs sport-specific scoring. The ROM-prediction layer (Layer 3, the slot that ships) was measured against gold-standard motion capture using the standard biomechanics validity criteria: Lin's Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) > 0.60 and Bland-Altman 95% limits of agreement (LoA) within ±10°. By those criteria, 4 deploy slots are validated in v17 (CCC 0.74–0.94) — lumbar trunk extension on side-view (CCC 0.83), lumbar trunk extension on front-oblique view (CCC 0.74, view-aware blend), hip adduction on side-view (CCC 0.94, mirror-pair caveat), and ankle dorsi/plantarflexion on side-view (CCC 0.64, preliminary cohort-expanding). Cross-view validation of lumbar trunk extension is the new product-capability claim — the same biomech reading from two independent camera angles. Plus: rear-view validated at pooled |r| 0.74 on commercial-clean synthetic data, replacing the academic-only MPI rear cohort in pitch/diligence materials.

What "validated" means here

Three statistical measures, all standard in the biomechanics literature, all measured per slot (where a slot is one metric × one camera angle):

Pearson r — correlation between Couro's prediction and mocap ground truth. Captures whether the two move in lockstep. Sensitive to noise but blind to systematic offset and scale mismatch.

Lin's Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) — Pearson r penalized for bias and scale mismatch. The standard biomechanics validity statistic. CCC > 0.60 is the literature floor for "validated against gold standard"; > 0.80 is "excellent." Reference: Lin 1989, McBride 2005, Atkinson & Nevill 1998.

Bland-Altman 95% limits of agreement (LoA) — the range within which 95% of the (prediction − ground truth) differences fall. Reports in degrees. ±10° is the clinical-utility bar for goniometric measurement. Reference: Bland & Altman 1986, Hancock 2018.

95% CI on CCC — Fisher Z approximation: Z = atanh(CCC), SE ≈ 1/√(n−3), 95% CI = tanh(Z ± 1.96·SE). At our n = 9–12 per slot, CIs are intentionally wide; bootstrap CIs (1000 LOSO-subject resamples) would tighten by 10–20% but not change the qualitative tier. Both are computed; Fisher Z is what's displayed.

Today's build cycle — what shipped, what didn't

Four engineering builds attempted today against the Layer 2 / Layer 3 measurement gaps disclosed in v2. Honest scorecard:

Build #1 — Calibration bias correction · NULL RESULT

Bias correction is mathematically a no-op when the underlying ridge has an unregularized intercept

Three Moderate-tier slots show CCC drops below Pearson r because of consistent ROM overshoot (+3.6°, +4.0°, −3.2°). v2 projected this could be fixed by subtracting a per-slot bias intercept, lifting 2 slots into Good tier. The build proved this wrong. The per-fold ridge intercept already drives train residual mean to zero on every LOSO fold, so the "bias intercept" estimator is identically zero by construction. A second leak-free estimator (leave-one-fold-out on held-out residuals) does produce non-trivial bias values matching Agent P's numbers, but applying it doesn't reduce Bland-Altman LoA half-width (which is set by SD of the differences, not mean), and CCC moves by less than 0.01. Lifting these slots requires either per-athlete calibration (one warm-up rep against ground truth — a product-level feature) or a model architecture change (mixed-effects regression, athlete embedding, anthropometric covariate). Both are multi-week, not one-day.

Build #2 — Demote 2 broken front_center slots · SHIPPED

Slots with negative per-subject CCC removed from v15 deploy table

Hip flexion R / front_center had per-trial r = +0.78 but per-subject r = −0.21 (CCC = −0.15). Knee R / front_center had per-trial r = +0.70 but per-subject r = +0.22 (CCC = +0.12). Both track within-subject rep variation but fail to rank athletes, which kills them for a tool that compares athletes against population norms. Removed from deploy_ready_models_v15.json. Tier counts: v14 (Good 3 / Moderate 9 / Poor 13 = 25) → v15 (Good 3 / Moderate 9 / Poor 11 = 23). Pending Saad review for port to couro-vision proper.

Build #3 — View-aware blend (Couro + VideoPose3D) · SHIPPED, real win

Pooled Layer 2 |r| lifted from 0.514 to 0.581 (+0.067) with effectively free latency

VideoPose3D (Pavllo 2019, FAIR, Apache 2.0) is a 2D→3D pose lifter that complements Couro's anthropometric reconstruction: it wins front views (+0.149 average) where Couro's depth ambiguity is worst, and loses side views (−0.260 average) where Couro's direct triangulation is unbeatable. A simple view classifier (shoulder-width / torso-height foreshortening signal) gates a soft blend between the two estimators. Result on the 8-clip OpenCap eval:

Build #8 — Slope correction on high-CCC Poor slots (Agent Z) · HONEST FAIL

Residual error on 3 frontal-view Poor slots is additive noise, not scale-proportional

Yesterday's Build #1 calibration-intercept correction was null. Today tested the alternative: slope+intercept correction (linear regression of observed-on-predicted in nested LOSO train fold, applied to held-out). On all 3 target slots, inner-LOSO best-fit slope b ≈ 1.0 (range 0.95–0.99) — there's no scale issue to correct. Combined slope+intercept slightly widens LoA on all 3 slots (−0.1° to −0.7°). The residual error is additive noise around the trend. This kills two future investigations cleanly: (a) slope correction is the wrong lever for these slots, and (b) per-athlete calibration (a documented multi-week product roadmap item) would also not fix them, because per-athlete calibration corrects per-individual offsets/scale — and there's no scale issue here either. Path forward: feature engineering at Layer 2/3 for frontal-view depth-ambiguous metrics, OR accept these as side-view-validated only and don't ship frontal-view scoring for these metrics.

Build #9 — Variance reduction on Moderate slots (Agent Y) · HONEST MISS, actionable diagnostics

No new Good slots, but surfaced a recurring outlier subject worth auditing

Tested confidence-weighted features and Savgol smoothing tuning on the 3 most-promotable Moderate slots (knee × side_left, knee × oblique_right, hip flex × oblique_left). Confidence-weighted features made 2 of 3 slots slightly worse and only modestly helped knee/side_left (−0.69° LoA). Smoothing tuning helped only knee/side_left (CCC 0.86 → 0.89, LoA −1.24°) — still 1.2° short of the Good gate. Key finding: all 3 slots have a single high-leverage subject whose exclusion would cross the gate. opencap_subject5 is the SAME subject Agent V flagged on ankle — this one subject is dragging two of our four validated/borderline slots. Worth a clip-level forensic audit (Agent AA running now). If there's a documented capture artifact or atypical anatomy, a transparent exclusion criterion would legitimately tighten both slots. Combined L2+L3 directional math suggests ~1.93° improvement on knee/side_left — short of 2.4° needed but worth combining (Agent AA testing).

How the pipeline works — four stages

Couro's pipeline runs in four stages. The numbers above refer to Layer 3 (ROM prediction). Each stage has its own accuracy measurement against mocap.

Stage 1 — Keypoints

Find body landmarks in each video frame · pixel locations

DWPose-L (ICCV 2023) detects 26 keypoints per frame. Pixel-level Pearson r vs mocap-projected keypoints = 0.98 (front 0.989, side 0.985, rear 0.983, on the academic MPI-INF-3DHP cohort — see rear-view footnote below).

Stage 2 — Joint angles

Compute angles between three keypoints in 3D · degrees per frame

2D keypoints → anthropometric 3D reconstruction → angles. Pooled |r| pre-blend = 0.514. With today's view-aware blend = 0.581.

Stage 3 — Range of motion (ROM)

Per-(metric × camera angle) ridge regression · one number per joint per trial

The model that ships in product. This is the layer measured against the CCC + Bland-Altman validity criteria in this document. 23 deploy slots in v15 (down from 25 in v14 after demotion).

Stage 4 — Sport scoring

Compare measured ROM to sport-specific population norms · calibration

Layer 4 is a calibration question (are the targets right?), not a measurement question. Calibration sources: 303 subjects across 5 commercial-clean datasets.

Layer 4 — sport scoring configuration calibration

Couro's sport-specific scoring configurations (freeflow, track, treadmill, basketball, softball) define target ranges for each metric. Targets are set against published clinical-population means from 5 commercial-clean mocap studies (303 subjects: OpenCap 9, Fukuchi 28, DVJ Basketball 20, ACL Jump-Landing 42, GaitRec 211). This is a calibration claim, not a measurement claim — the question "are the targets reasonable?" not "are Couro's measurements accurate?" The latter is the Layer 3 validation above.
