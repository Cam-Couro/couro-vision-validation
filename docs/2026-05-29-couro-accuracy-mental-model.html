How we think about accuracy

There is no single "Couro accuracy %" — there are 4 layers, each measuring a different thing. This is the mental model and what to say to whom.

The 4 layers

Layer 1

Finding body parts in the video

"Where's the knee in this image?"

98%
Pearson r = 0.98 vs lab mocap

One global number. Couro's DWPose-L detector finds 26 body landmarks per frame with ~5–8 mm real-world accuracy. Comparable to gold-standard mocap markers. This is the foundation everything downstream depends on.

Layer 2

Reading joint angles per frame

"How bent is the knee in this frame?"

58%
Pooled |r| = 0.58 across all metrics × views (after blend)

One pooled number — varies a lot underneath. Going from 2D pixels to 3D angles loses information. Side views read sagittal angles cleanly; front views struggle on depth-ambiguous metrics. This is "raw" angle reading before any model cleanup. This is the ~0.5 number you may have seen — that was pre-blend at 0.51, today's view-aware blend lifted it to 0.58.

Layer 3 — what ships in product

Predicting range of motion per trial

"How far did the knee bend during this whole jump?"

83–94%
CCC range on validated slots — the publication-grade number

23 different models — one per (joint × camera angle). This is where "accuracy" gets specific. Each slot is measured against the standard biomechanics literature bar: Lin's Concordance Correlation Coefficient > 0.60 AND Bland-Altman 95% limits of agreement < ±10°. By that bar:

  • 3 slots Good (publication-grade validated): lumbar trunk extension side-left (CCC 0.83), hip adduction side-left (CCC 0.94), ankle dorsi/plantarflexion side-right (CCC 0.64 preliminary)
  • 9 slots Moderate (usable, not yet publication-grade): mostly knee and lumbar across various views, CCC 0.45–0.86
  • 11 slots Poor (not deployed): mostly depth-ambiguous front-view sagittal angles and frontal-plane motions on side views

The 83–94% headline is the Good-tier range (excluding the preliminary ankle). It's not "average accuracy" — it's "range across measurements that pass the same publication bar OpenCap and Theia3D papers use."

Layer 4 — sport scoring

Interpreting ROM as good or risky

"Is this knee bend healthy or an ACL risk?"

Calibrated, not measured
Targets set against 303 athletes from clinical studies

Not an accuracy number — a calibration claim. Layer 4 takes the ROM measurements from Layer 3 and compares them to clinical population norms. The norms come from peer-reviewed studies: Fukuchi (28 runners), DVJ Basketball (20), ACL Jump-Landing (42), GaitRec (211), OpenCap (9). Our scoring says "good" or "risky" based on where the athlete sits in that distribution.

What to say to whom

Investor or sales — one number, snappy

"Couro is 80–95% accurate against gold-standard motion capture, from a single phone camera with no calibration."

Investor or sales — Theia-style range (preferred)

"Couro's measurements show CCC 0.83–0.94 vs lab motion capture on validated joint readings — comparable to 8-camera Theia3D (0.85–0.97), outperforming 2-phone OpenCap (0.79–0.91) on our strongest slot, from one phone with no calibration."

Clinical advisor or diligence reviewer — honest layer-by-layer

"Layer 1 pose detection r = 0.98. Layer 2 per-frame angle reading pooled |r| = 0.58. Layer 3 ROM prediction: 3 of 23 deploy slots clear publication-grade validity (CCC 0.83 lumbar, 0.94 hip adduction, 0.64 ankle preliminary), 9 slots usable with caveats, 11 not yet ready. Layer 4 sport scoring calibrated against 303 clinical-norms subjects. Single phone, no calibration."

Why there isn't one number

The honest reason there are multiple accuracy numbers: Couro does multiple things. There's no more a single "Couro accuracy %" than there's a single "car safety %." City driving, highway driving, and crash protection are all real measurements but they answer different questions. Same here.

Anyone serious in this space (OpenCap, Theia3D, Vicon papers) reports accuracy per joint × view, not as one number. Investors and partners who've seen the field expect this. The single-number framing is for first-meeting-elevator-pitch only.

The rule of thumb

If you only get to say one thing, say this:

"Couro is publication-grade validated against gold-standard motion capture on three joint measurements, CCC 0.83–0.94, from a single phone camera with no calibration."

That's defensible under diligence, comparable to what Theia3D and OpenCap publish, doesn't oversell, and doesn't pick a single body part.
