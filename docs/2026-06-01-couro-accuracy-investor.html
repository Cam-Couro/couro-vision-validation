The opportunity

Biomechanics has been locked behind $50–80K lab rigs.

Gold-standard movement analysis — the kind that flags injury risk and quantifies mechanics — requires a Vicon lab (10+ infrared cameras, reflective markers, $50K+) or a markerless install like Theia3D (8 cameras, $80K+). Even the open-source standard, OpenCap, needs two calibrated phones.

Couro delivers comparable validity from a single phone, with no calibration step — putting lab-grade movement intelligence in the hands of any coach, trainer, or athlete with a camera in their pocket.

The proof

Validated against motion capture — on commercially licensed data.

Every number below is measured against gold-standard mocap using Lin's Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) and Bland-Altman limits of agreement — the standard biomechanics validity criteria (CCC > 0.60 = validated; > 0.80 = excellent). Why it's hard to copy

The same reading from wherever the phone sits.

Couro validated trunk extension from two independent camera angles — side and front-oblique. That means a coach can place the phone wherever the sport allows and still get a publication-grade reading. Cross-view robustness is exactly what biomechanics reviewers look for, and it's the difference between a lab demo and a product that survives the real world.

Strategic unlock

Rear-view — the angle that matters for pitching and gait.

The posterior view is the most valuable angle for running gait, softball pitching, and posterior-chain assessment.
