"Couro reads body movement from a single phone camera at publication-grade validity (CCC 0.79–0.94) across nine joint measurements — covering trunk, hip adduction, hip flexion, and knee — measured against gold-standard motion capture using the Bland-Altman framework the OpenCap and Theia3D papers report under. Trunk extension is validated from five camera angles (both sides, both front-obliques, and front-center) and knee flexion is validated from two angles. Comparable to 2-phone OpenCap (0.79–0.91) and approaching 8-camera Theia3D (0.85–0.97) — from one phone instead of two or eight, with no calibration setup."

What we deliberately don't claim

  • 9 of 23 deploy slots are Tier 1. 3 more are Tier 2 supplementary. The remaining 11 are explicitly Moderate (usable with caveats) or Poor (not ready). Tier labels are reported, not averaged.
  • Hip adduction 0.94 has a flagged mirror twin. Side-right scored 0.27 in the same study — view-specific keypoint detector asymmetry confirmed (mirror-flip augmentation didn't move it). Disclosed anywhere the 0.94 number appears.
  • Learned Layer 2 slots (v18, v20, v23, v24, v26, v27, v29, v31, v33, v38) carry an L2-LOSO upper-bound caveat. Layer 2 was trained on all 9 OpenCap subjects (or 24 OC+ASP) with subject-level LOSO at Layer 3 only. Strict double-LOSO would likely reduce CCC by 0.05–0.10 on the most-promoted slots.
  • Residual calibration (v38) adds a per-slot (slope, intercept) correction at inference. Fit without leakage via nested LOSO on training subjects. Ship-ready and disclosed.
  • All n's are small (n = 9 LOSO subjects). Cohort expansion is the next priority for any clinical-trial-grade claim.
  • "No calibration" means no per-session setup. The Layer 4 calibration that maps biomech ROM to sport-specific risk scores was trained on a separate 303-subject cohort and is part of the deployed model — but no end-user calibration is required.
