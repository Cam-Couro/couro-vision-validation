Couro vs Vicon — DWPose backbone test: r ceiling 0.83 → 0.85, broken slots rescued

v13 · 2026-05-28 · Head-to-head: RTMPose-X Halpe-26 (v12) vs DWPose-L COCO-WholeBody (v13) · same 26 subjects · 1,680 trials · LOSO cross-validated

We swapped the keypoint backbone on the 17 ASPset-510 subjects from RTMPose-X Halpe-26 to DWPose-L (COCO-WholeBody, 133 keypoints with explicit foot supervision) and re-ran the v12 regression head-to-head. Same training pipeline, same features, same LOSO splits — only the keypoint extractor changed.

Result is complementary, not a clean sweep. DWPose wins 6 of 20 trainable slots, RTMPose-X holds 6, the other 8 are non-v12 winners. The picker now selects whichever backbone is better per slot. Ceiling r moved 0.83 → 0.85. The worst v12 deploy slot (lumbar extension × side_right at r=+0.07) got rescued to r=+0.49 — a 7× improvement.

Net: 23 deploy slots, 1 do-not-ship, same totals as v12 — but the deploys are stronger and the failing edge cases are fewer.

1. The two backbones — what each brings

RTMPose-X Halpe-26 v12

Output 26 keypoints (body + 6 feet)
Training Halpe + Body7 (general human pose)
Foot supervision Auxiliary — 6 of 26 outputs
Strength Interior body angles (hip, knee, lumbar) — broadest training distribution
Weakness Foot keypoints noisier; weakest at oblique-side foot occlusions
Backbone Couro production model — same weights ship today

DWPose-L UCoco-384 v13

Output 133 keypoints (body + feet + face + hands)
Training COCO-WholeBody + UBody distillation
Foot supervision First-class — full distillation from wholebody teacher
Strength Foot/ankle stability; better signal on side views
Weakness Slightly lower body-only ROM precision (more capacity spread across face+hands)
Architecture Same SimCC head as RTMPose — drop-in inference

For Halpe-26 compatibility, the DWPose 133-kp output is remapped: 17 body + 6 feet map directly; head_top is extrapolated from eyes+nose; neck and hip_center are midpoints of shoulders / hips. So downstream feature extraction (anthropometric 3D reconstruction, joint angles) is identical.

5. The pattern: where each backbone is structurally better

View familyDWPose winsRTMPose winsWhy

This pattern is what you'd predict from first principles: DWPose's foot supervision is most valuable in views where the foot contributes signal. We didn't engineer this finding — it fell out of running the head-to-head.

9. Why DWPose isn't a clean sweep — the honest read

The naive expectation going in was "more keypoints + better foot training = better everywhere." The reality is more interesting:

  1. Foot keypoints help only where feet carry signal. From dead-front, the foot's position adds nothing in-plane that hip-knee don't already encode. From the side, the foot is the vertical reference and the noise term — DWPose dominates.
  2. Model capacity is a finite resource. DWPose spreads its representation across body+face+hands+feet. RTMPose-X Halpe-26 is a body-only specialist. For pure interior body angles seen straight-on, the specialist wins.
  3. The per-slot picker is the right design. Rather than choosing one backbone for all 25 slots, we now pick per slot. Future backbone improvements layer in cleanly — same picker, more candidates.

The deployment shift is that we now ship two ONNX models (RTMPose-X for 6 slots, DWPose-L for 6 slots) plus the existing OpenCap-only models for the other 13. Per-clip inference cost roughly doubles (one pass each) for the 12 ASPset-trained slots; the picker selects at training time, not inference time, so production stays simple.

12. What's still left as a real lever

  1. OpenCap × DWPose rerun — would unblock the ankle slots (currently r=0.17 and r=−0.15) since DWPose's foot keypoints would replace RTMPose's on the OpenCap drop-jump trials where ankle GT exists. ~$0.50 of additional AWS compute. Likely the single highest-ROI next step for ankle.
  2. Sport-specific data collection — ASPset is generic athletic motions. AUSL softball pitching, Sharks hockey skating, etc. still need their own validation. Custom mocap or AthletePose3D commercial license.
  3. Backbone fine-tuning on athletic motion — both RTMPose-X and DWPose-L were trained on general human pose. A sport-supervised fine-tune (even on synthetic + AMASS) would likely lift the front_center slots that didn't move in v13.
  4. Multi-camera fusion — out of scope per product constraints, but would push best r toward 0.90+.
