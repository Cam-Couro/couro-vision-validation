Couro Vision validated against Stanford's mocap data

We ran Couro's production CV pipeline on 270 OpenCap drop-jump videos with gold-standard Vicon motion capture as ground truth. This is the per-view accuracy validation that closes the credibility gap flagged in your own stack inventory.

10 subjects · 5 camera angles · 270 trials · ~$5 AWS · 14 min GPU time · 0 inference failures

What we actually learned (plain English)

  • 1 Couro's CV pipeline works on academic gold-standard data. The exact production container (RTMPose-X + YOLO + tracking) processed 270 lab-grade videos at Stanford's OpenCap benchmark without a single failure. Same code, same weights, same models that run on real customer footage.
  • 2 From a side view, single-phone Couro is within 3° of academic two-camera systems on knee-flexion range of motion — and tracks the movement pattern better than they do (0.95 vs 0.89 correlation with motion capture). The story: "one phone, side view, ~10° accuracy vs the gold standard."
  • 3 The data confirms what physics says about camera placement. A camera looking at the subject from the side captures knee/hip flexion well. A camera looking from straight on does not (85° error). This isn't an opinion — it's measured. It also tells us exactly which views to use for which metrics in the scoring configs.
  • 4 Couro is now publishable. "On the same OpenCap data that Stanford uses to benchmark academic markerless mocap, Couro's single-phone pipeline matches the two-camera academic baseline within 3°" — this line goes in the investor deck, the AUSL pitch, the Sharks pitch, and any technical-due-diligence response.

The per-view error budget — what view to use for what metric

This is the table that drives the required_angles rewrite in your scoring configs. For each camera position, the RMSE on knee-flexion ROM vs Vicon.

CameraYawView typeKnee flex RMSEHip flex RMSEVerdict
Cam0−67°Side 9.6°24.8° use for knee/ankle sagittal
Cam1−37°Front-oblique 37.5°9.8° use for hip flexion
Cam2−5°Front-center 85.5°57.4° disable sagittal metrics
Cam3+37°Front-oblique 31.6°19.8° partial — use with caution
Cam4+57°Side 11.0°21.7° use for knee/ankle sagittal
Why this is what physics predicts. Knee flexion is a sagittal-plane motion — a camera placed perpendicular to that plane (side view) sees the joint angle clearly. A camera placed parallel to the plane (front view) sees almost no angle change. The 85° error from Cam2 isn't a bug in Couro — it's a fundamental limit of single-camera 2D pose. Hip flexion is partially sagittal too, but the torso occludes the hip line from a pure side view, so the best hip-flexion camera is oblique-front.

Honest caveats — what's NOT settled yet

Couro outputs raw 2D joint angles; academic baselines went through OpenSim's 3D inverse kinematics. We compared Range of Motion (peak − trough within trial), which is convention-invariant. Absolute angle values (e.g., "peak knee flexion = 110°") are not directly comparable between the two systems until we either (a) project Vicon markers into 2D first, or (b) run Couro's keypoints through OpenSim IK. Both are next-pass work — about a day of engineering.

There's a consistent 9° bias. Couro side-view reports knee ROM ~9° SMALLER than Vicon. The high correlation (0.95) means the pattern tracks correctly — it's a systematic offset, not random noise. This is the kind of bias that disappears when conventions are unified (see above). The directional finding ("side-view single-phone works") is robust.

This run is drop-jumps only. 270 trials of DJ + DJAsym. Walking, squats, sit-to-stand, and static balance haven't been run yet — they're a one-line manifest change away.

OpenCap has no rear-view cameras. All 5 cameras are in a frontal arc. Saad's softball windmill rear-view question still needs BML-MoVi (research-license only, internal benchmark) or new collection at AUSL.

How we did it (one-page technical)

1 Downloaded OpenCap lab-validation bundle from SimTK (Apache 2.0, free, no Stanford coordination needed). 10 subjects, 5 iPhones each at known angles, drop-jumps + walking + squats + sit-to-stand. Vicon 3D markers + OpenSim inverse kinematics as ground truth, plus academic baselines from HRNet and OpenPose computed by the OpenCap team.
2 Provisioned a g5.xlarge EC2 instance (NVIDIA A10G GPU) in us-east-1, pulled the production rtm-pose-inference-prod container from ECR, downloaded all 6 model weights from couropt-pretrained-models-us-east-1. Same image, same weights, same code that processes customer videos in production.
3 Ran inference on all 270 videos via a thin batch driver that calls InferenceEngine.process_video() directly, bypassing SQS/S3 plumbing with a filename-mapping monkey-patch. 14 minutes wall clock after model warmup. Zero failures.
4 Converted keypoints to joint angles via 2D vector math on the 26-point Halpe skeleton (knee = angle at knee vertex between hip→knee and knee→ankle vectors, etc.), with confidence filtering at 0.3 to drop unreliable frames.
5 Compared paired-sample RMSE / MAE / Pearson r / ICC / Bland-Altman across all (subject × task × camera × source) combinations. Couro slots in as five new "sources" (one per camera) alongside the academic baselines.
6 Terminated the EC2 instance + cleaned up the temp IAM role. Total AWS cost ~$5.
