Refining Couro for non-side camera views

Round-2 of OpenCap validation. We added anthropometric-foreshortening 3D recovery to lift front- and oblique-view Couro outputs out of the "unusable" zone. Front-view knee-flexion error dropped from 85° to 52° — a 40% reduction with one day of work and no new ML training.

270 trials reanalyzed · 10 subjects · 5 cameras × 2 algorithms compared · ~$0 additional spend (all local CPU)

What we just learned (plain English)

  • 1 The front-camera problem is partially fixable, and we fixed it. Original front-view knee error vs Vicon was 85° (effectively unusable). With the new method, it's 52° — still worse than a side camera but back in the "measurable with caveats" range. Same Couro keypoints, smarter post-processing.
  • 2 The fix uses biomechanics, not new ML. When the leg points toward the camera, the thigh and shank appear shorter in the image than they really are. Knowing the subject's height and the camera's intrinsics, we can recover the out-of-plane tilt from how foreshortened the segment is. This is the same trick OpenSim IK uses — implemented in 200 lines of numpy.
  • 3 For hip flexion, the new method wins on 4 out of 5 cameras. Hip joints move out of the image plane during squats and jumps, so foreshortening recovery is a clean win. Cam1 (front-oblique) is now within 10° RMSE of mocap on hip flexion — comparable to academic multi-camera systems.
  • 4 Production should pick the algorithm per camera position. Side cameras → naive 2D (no foreshortening to recover). Front and oblique cameras → anthropometric 3D recovery. This per-view algorithm choice is now codified in the harness and ready for Saad's required_angles rewrite.

How the fix works

When a leg bends toward or away from a camera, the 2D projection of the limb gets shorter without the joint angle changing in pixels. The naive vector math interprets "shorter pixels" as "smaller angle" — wrong. The anthropometric method asks "given that the femur is exactly 24% of body height (so 47cm for a 1.96m subject), and given the camera's known focal length and the subject's distance, how shortened is the femur in the image vs reality?" That foreshortening ratio gives the out-of-plane tilt magnitude (in degrees) via arccos.

Combine the in-plane angle (from 2D pixels) with the out-of-plane tilt (from foreshortening), and you have the 3D joint angle. We do this per segment per frame.

For each joint, we then smoothly blend between the naive 2D estimate and the anthropometric 3D estimate based on how foreshortened the segments actually are. When both segments are seen in profile (side view) → trust the 2D angle. When one or both are foreshortened (front view) → trust the 3D estimate. The result is a hybrid algorithm that picks the right tool per-joint per-frame.

The unfixable part. Without temporal smoothing or a second camera, we can't disambiguate "the knee is moving forward toward the camera" from "the knee is moving backward away from the camera" — both produce identical 2D images. This sign-ambiguity is what keeps front-view knee flexion at 51° instead of dropping closer to the ~15° side-view number. To fully close the gap from one camera, the next steps are either (a) a 2D→3D pose-lifting deep model like MotionBert, (b) full OpenSim IK with temporal smoothing, or (c) adding a second phone. Each is a 1-2 week piece of work.

What this means for product decisions

  1. The "must use side view" constraint relaxes. Front/oblique cameras can now produce hip-flexion measurements competitive with multi-camera academic systems (~10° RMSE from oblique). The "place your phone wherever" pitch becomes more honest — within an acknowledged per-view accuracy budget.
  2. Front-center camera still shouldn't drive risk scores. Even with the v2 fix, knee/hip flexion from a directly-facing camera carry 48-52° error. That's too much noise for scoring. Use front-center only for symmetry checks and frontal-plane metrics like knee valgus.
  3. Per-camera algorithm selection is now a real config option. The harness exposes both Couro_TrackMode_* (naive 2D) and Couro_Anthro3D_* (hybrid) as data sources. Saad can wire scoring to pick the best one per-metric per-view automatically.
  4. The next round of work is clear. Either OpenSim IK with temporal smoothing (resolves sign ambiguity) or a small 2D→3D lifting model. Both are 1-2 weeks. Both should drop front-view error toward the 15-25° range — making single-phone front-view a fully production-grade source.
