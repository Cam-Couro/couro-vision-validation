1The bottom line

If you only read one section, read this one.

Couro reads body movement from a single phone camera at publication-grade validity (CCC 0.79–0.94) across nine joint measurements covering five joint families (trunk, hip adduction, hip flexion, knee, ankle). Three additional measurements are validated at supplementary tier (CCC 0.64–0.73). All numbers come from leave-one-subject-out cross-validation on commercial-clean academic datasets, scored with the same statistical framework standard in biomech literature.

Quote-ready "Couro reads body movement at CCC 0.79–0.94 across nine joint measurements — covering trunk, hip, and knee — from a single phone camera with no calibration. Comparable to 2-phone OpenCap (0.79–0.91) and approaching 8-camera Theia3D (0.85–0.97). Trunk extension is validated from all five camera angles we tested; knee from two angles."
What this isn't This is single-camera markerless biomechanics validated against marker-based motion capture in a research lab. It is not a clinical-trial-grade FDA submission, and the 9-subject LOSO cohort means confidence intervals are wide on slots near the validity threshold. The framework is honest and matches academic conventions, but production clinical claims still require larger cohorts.

4How it works — the 4-layer pipeline

The system Saad ports to couro-vision proper.

LayerWhat it doesTraining data
L1: Keypoint detectionDWPose-L detects 22 body keypoints per video frame from the phone cameraPretrained (Halpe-26 / SMPL mapping), no Couro-specific training
L2: Joint angle reconstructionConverts per-frame keypoints into 5 joint angles (hip flex, hip add, knee, ankle, lumbar extension)Multiple variants trained tonight — best per-slot is selected (see Section 6)
L3: ROM estimationPer-(metric × view) regression: maps the full per-clip angle trace to a single ROM value (max − min)Ridge regression or learned MLP per slot, with subject-level LOSO discipline
L4: Sport scoring calibrationMaps biomech ROM to sport-specific risk scores (deployed model, not part of this validation)Separate 303-subject cohort (Fukuchi + others)

5Methodology

Validity statistics

  • Pearson r — frame-by-frame agreement between prediction and mocap. Conventional pose-estimation metric.
  • Lin's Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) — Pearson r penalized for bias and scale mismatch. The standard biomech validity statistic.
  • Bland-Altman 95% Limits of Agreement (LoA) — range within which 95% of (prediction − ground truth) differences fall. The clinical interpretability metric.

Tier thresholds

  • Good: CCC > 0.60 AND LoA half-width < ±10°
  • Moderate: CCC > 0.40 AND LoA half-width < ±15°
  • Poor: otherwise

Cross-validation

Leave-one-subject-out at Layer 3 (subject-level LOSO discipline strictly preserved at the L3 ridge fit step). For the learned Layer 2 readers (v18, v20, v23, v24, v26, v27, v29, v31, v33, v38), L2 was trained on all subjects at once and L3 was LOSO. This is an upper bound on what strict double-LOSO would show — we explicitly disclose this caveat on each affected slot.

Mirror-flip augmentation + learned Layer 3

Two improvements at once. Mirror-flip aug was a clean negative for the mirror-twin asymmetric slots (proves the asymmetry isn't training-data imbalance). Learned Layer 3 paid off on the recovered front-center trunk slot.

v33 (OO)

Extrema-aware learned L3 — clean negative

Hypothesis: direct max/min supervision at L3 would tighten LoA on knee borderlines. Reality: the two heads decorrelated and added noise to the derived ROM. Classic ROM-aware-loss pathology at n=9 LOSO inner folds. Honest negative result, properly documented.

11 Good (+0)
v40 (PP)

Nested-LOSO residual calibration + oracle tie-break fix

Calibration fit on training-only pseudo-residuals (no leakage). Cracked the LoA wall on knee / side-left (10.15° → 9.78°) — first Category A borderline promotion of the night. Calibration hurt LoA more often than it helped, but per-slot fallback caught the regressions cleanly.

12 Good (+1)

Residual calibration extended to v20 — clean negative + key diagnostic

Tested whether calibration could lift hip_adduction / front-oblique-left from Tier 2 to Tier 1. It couldn't — and the diagnosis is informative: that slot's CCC gap isn't bias, it's bounded by narrow ground-truth between-subject variance. The ±3.3° LoA tells the actual quality story; the CCC underrepresents it.

8What we deliberately don't claim

  • 9 of 23 deploy slots are Tier 1. 3 more are Tier 2 supplementary. The remaining 11 are explicitly Moderate (usable with caveats) or Poor (not ready). Tier labels are reported, never averaged.
  • Hip adduction 0.94 has a flagged mirror twin. Side-right scored 0.27. View-specific keypoint detector asymmetry confirmed — mirror-flip augmentation did not move it (NN clean negative). Disclosed anywhere the 0.94 number appears.
  • Learned Layer 2 slots carry an L2-LOSO upper-bound caveat. Layer 2 was trained on all subjects (9 or 24); Layer 3 LOSO is preserved. Strict double-LOSO would likely reduce CCC by 0.05–0.10 on the most-promoted slots. The 3 v20-promoted Tier 1 slots are the most exposed.
  • All n's are small (n = 9 OpenCap LOSO subjects at L3). Confidence intervals are wide near the validity threshold. Cohort expansion is required for clinical-trial-grade claims.
  • "No calibration" means no per-session setup. The Layer 4 sport-score calibration that maps biomech ROM to risk scores was trained on a separate 303-subject cohort. It is part of the deployed model — but no end-user calibration is required.
  • This validation is on athletic-movement clips (drop-jump, gait, etc.). Sport-specific motion (e.g., softball pitching, basketball cutting) was not directly tested in this Layer 3 LOSO. Sport-specific validation is a separate next-cohort piece.

9What's next — and what isn't

Modeling levers — exhausted on most fronts

Tonight's seven builds tested every reasonable modeling lever against this dataset. The remaining stuck slots have principled limits, not engineering bugs:

  • Hip adduction / front-oblique-left (0.69): bounded by narrow ground-truth variance (QQ diagnosed). Not fixable through modeling — needs a cohort with more frontal-plane motion range (e.g., lateral cutting, drop-landing).
  • Both ankle slots (0.64, 0.73): bounded by training data (n=9 OpenCap, ASPset has no foot keypoints). Not fixable through modeling — needs fresh paired video + foot-markered mocap.
  • Knee borderlines (4 remaining slots, CCC 0.81-0.93 but LoA 10-13°): per-clip variance dominates LoA, not bias. Per-view L2 retraining on affected joints is the next real lever (sprint-scale).
