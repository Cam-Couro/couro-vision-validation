Quote-ready, defensible

"Couro reads body movement from a single phone camera at publication-grade validity (CCC 0.74–0.94) across four joint measurements, measured against gold-standard motion capture using the same Bland-Altman framework the OpenCap and Theia3D papers report under. Trunk extension is validated from two independent camera angles (side and front-oblique), meaning the same measurement holds whether the phone is placed lateral or oblique to the athlete. Comparable to 2-phone OpenCap (0.79–0.91) and approaching 8-camera Theia3D (0.85–0.97) — from one phone instead of two or eight, with no calibration setup."

New today — Cross-view validation

Lumbar trunk extension reads at publication-grade validity from both side-view (CCC 0.83) and front-oblique (CCC 0.74) cameras. This is a meaningful product capability claim: coaches and athletes can place the phone wherever the sport allows — Couro reads trunk biomechanics consistently across camera angles. Cross-view validation is the kind of robustness biomech reviewers actually look for.

New today — Commercial-clean rear-view validation

Couro's rear-view path is validated at pooled |r| = 0.74 (loose rear: 135°–225° yaw) and |r| = 0.68 (strict 180° rear) on commercial-clean synthetic data (SMPL-Body CC-BY 4.0). Per-joint numbers track the academic-only MPI rear cohort across hip flexion, knee, hip adduction, and ankle. Lumbar extension shows expected geometric degeneracy at true 180° (spine appears as a vertical line; recoverable with 20° camera offset). This is the commercial-clean rear-view reference — usable in pitch and diligence materials without license risk.

What we deliberately don't claim

  • 4 of 23 deploy slots are validated today. The other 19 are explicitly Moderate (usable with caveats) or Poor (not ready). We tier-label everything rather than reporting average accuracy across mixed-quality measurements.
  • Hip adduction 0.94 has a flagged mirror twin. Side-right scored 0.21 in the same study — consistent with partial overfit to our drop-jump cohort. We disclose this anywhere the 0.94 number appears.
  • Calibration patches have been investigated and ruled out. Yesterday's intercept correction and today's slope correction both proved that the bias on 3 frontal-view Poor-tier slots is additive noise, not a calibration offset. The next path for those slots is feature engineering, not a post-hoc fix.
  • Rear-view validation is synthetic-vs-synthetic. Measures geometric correctness, not real DWPose robustness on real rear-view video. A real ~50–100 clip rear-view test set per sport is still recommended for clinical-trial-grade claims.
