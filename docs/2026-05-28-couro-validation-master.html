What Couro is validated to do

Couro takes a video from a single phone, runs a pose-estimation model, reconstructs the athlete's joint angles, and outputs sport-specific biomechanical metrics and risk scores. We have measured the accuracy of this pipeline at four distinct stages against gold-standard motion capture, across 575+ athletes from 8 independent peer-reviewed academic datasets. Each stage has its own validation number; together they describe an end-to-end system that correlates with lab-grade mocap at r=0.78–0.91 on the metrics most clinically useful for sport screening (hip/knee flexion), with sport-specific scoring configurations calibrated to within 0.13σ of population means — all from a single phone camera with no calibration.

Important note on the headline numbers. Couro reports two correlation-style accuracy numbers, and they measure different things in the pipeline. Both are real, both are validated.

"92–95% composite alignment" from the March 2026 autoresearch is the calibration of our scoring targets against population means (z-error 0.13σ across 42 metrics, 548 subjects). It answers: "is the benchmark right?"

"r = 0.85" or "r = 0.91" from May 2026 multiview is the per-trial prediction accuracy against mocap on a specific (metric × camera angle) combination. It answers: "when an athlete uploads a video, how close is the reading to the truth on that trial?"

Both layers are independently validated. The doc below walks through all four pipeline stages.

The four validation layers

Couro's pipeline runs in four stages: video → keypoints → joint angles → ROM features → sport scoring. Each stage has its own independent validation against motion capture ground truth.

Layer 2 — Joint angle accuracy

What's the actual joint angle in degrees?

4.75° MAE (was 7.19°, 34% improvement after signal processing)

Once keypoints are detected, Couro reconstructs joint angles in 3D using anthropometric scaling and a signal-processing pipeline (Kalman filter + Butterworth smoothing, parameters optimized via autoresearch). We measured joint angle reading vs mocap angle across 30 video-GT pairs in 150 experiments, against Fukuchi RBDS mocap.

Layer 3 — Per-camera-angle ROM prediction

How accurate is each metric × camera angle combination?

r = 0.41–0.91 · RMSE 5–17° · 24 of 25 deploy slots ship

Couro deploys 25 ridge regression models, one per (metric × camera angle) combination. Each takes joint angle features from a video and predicts the athlete's specific ROM for that trial. Validated using leave-one-subject-out cross-validation against 27 athletes with paired video + mocap from OpenCap, ASPset-510, and (added tonight) MPI-INF-3DHP.
